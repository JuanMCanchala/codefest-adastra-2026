# Governance of autonomous weapons systems

Autonomous weapons systems raise unresolved questions about accountability and
compliance with international humanitarian law. States developing lethal
autonomous capabilities face pressure to keep meaningful human control over the
use of force. The Geneva Conventions remain the reference framework.

Multilateral discussions have not produced a binding treaty, and the pace of
military artificial intelligence adoption outstrips regulation. Analysts warn
that an arms race in autonomous systems could lower the threshold for conflict
and complicate attribution after an incident. Confidence-building measures and
export controls are proposed as partial mitigations while a broader consensus is
negotiated among major powers and regional blocs.
