# Space debris and the sustainability of Low Earth Orbit

The proliferation of artificial objects in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) threatens the
long-term sustainability of space operations. Defunct satellites, spent rocket
stages and collision fragments increase the probability of cascading collisions,
a scenario known as the Kessler syndrome.

Analytical products from space agencies and multilateral organisms track the
population of debris larger than ten centimeters. These reports do not contain
raw orbital data, but strategic assessments of collision risk and mitigation.

Prospective scenarios point to active debris removal and stricter end-of-life
disposal rules as the main levers for a stable orbital environment. The economic
value of the LEO shell depends on preserving a low collision rate.
