# Territorial governance and social perception in Latin America

Territorial dynamics in Latin America and the Caribbean have strategic
implications for the security and stability of states. Governance gaps in remote
regions create space for illicit economies and organized violence, which in turn
erode institutional trust and social cohesion.

Social perception of institutions varies widely across countries and shapes
political stability. Youth unemployment, unequal access to education and forced
displacement are recurrent drivers of unrest. Comparative analysis of regional
and international open sources, contrasted with Colombian academic observatories,
helps identify emerging patterns, risks and strategic opportunities for the
region.
